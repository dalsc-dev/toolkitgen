What Is eDPI?

eDPI (Effective Dots Per Inch) is a standardized measurement that combines your mouse's hardware DPI with your in-game sensitivity multiplier. It provides a single number that represents your true effective sensitivity, making it easy to compare settings across different games and players.

The eDPI Formula

eDPI = DPI × In-Game Sensitivity

For example, if you play at 800 DPI with an in-game sensitivity of 0.5, your eDPI is:

800 × 0.5 = 400 eDPI

Understanding Mouse DPI

DPI (Dots Per Inch) measures how many pixels your cursor moves for each inch you move your mouse. Higher DPI means the cursor moves more pixels per inch of physical movement.

Common DPI Settings

  • 400 DPI: Low sensitivity, requires more physical movement
  • 800 DPI: Very popular among pro gamers
  • 1600 DPI: Medium-high sensitivity
  • 3200 DPI: High sensitivity, requires less physical movement

DPI vs. CPI

DPI and CPI (Counts Per Inch) are technically the same thing. Mouse manufacturers often use CPI in specifications, but DPI has become the common term.

Polling Rate

Polling rate (measured in Hz) is how often your mouse reports its position to your computer:

  • 500 Hz: Updates every 2ms
  • 1000 Hz: Updates every 1ms
  • 4000 Hz: Updates every 0.25ms (high-end gaming mice)

Understanding In-Game Sensitivity

In-game sensitivity is a multiplier applied to your raw DPI. Different games have different sensitivity scales, which is why the same DPI can feel completely different across games.

Finding Your Perfect Sensitivity

Precision Aiming

If you prioritize long-range precision (sniping, tactical shooters), consider lower eDPI with large mouse movements.

The Science of Mouse Acceleration

What Is Mouse Acceleration?

Mouse acceleration (also called "pointer precision") makes cursor movement depend on how fast you move your mouse, not just distance.

Why Pro Gamers Disable It

Acceleration adds unpredictability. The same physical movement can produce different cursor movements depending on speed.

How to Disable Acceleration

  • Windows: Settings > Mouse > Additional options > Disable "Enhance pointer precision"
  • Game settings: Most competitive games have acceleration disabled by default
  • Registry: Advanced users can modify Windows mouse parameters

Mouse Pad Surface and Sensitivity

Cloth Pads

Cloth pads offer consistent friction and are the most popular among pro gamers. Work well with any sensitivity.

Hard Pads

Hard pads offer lower friction and faster movement. May require adjustment if switching from cloth.

Glass/Ceramic Pads

Glass pads offer extremely fast, consistent movement. Require adaptation period due to very low friction.

Matching Sensitivity to Surface

Lower friction surfaces may feel like higher sensitivity. If switching surfaces, slight sensitivity adjustments may help.

Sensor Types and Performance

Optical Sensors

Optical sensors use LED light to track movement on surfaces. Modern optical sensors are highly accurate and popular in gaming mice.

Laser Sensors

Laser sensors use laser light and can track on more surfaces. Some gamers report "acceleration-like" behavior with laser sensors.

Hero Sensors (Logitech)

HERO sensors offer 1:1 tracking with no smoothing or acceleration across the entire DPI range.

Focus+ Sensors (Razer)

Focus+ sensors also claim 1:1 tracking with intelligent tracking for reduced drift.

Common Gaming Sensitivity Mistakes

Mistake 1: Copying Pros Exactly

Problem: Pro settings may not suit your grip, setup, or playstyle.

Solution: Use pro eDPI as a starting reference, then adjust to your preference.

Mistake 2: Sensitivity Too High

Problem: Difficulty making precise adjustments at distance.

Solution: Lower sensitivity and practice controlling your crosshair with larger movements.

Mistake 3: Sensitivity Too Low

Problem: Getting flanked because you can't turn fast enough.

Solution: Increase sensitivity to a level where you can comfortably perform 180-degree turns.

Mistake 4: Inconsistent Settings

Problem: Different sensitivity across games disrupts muscle memory.

Solution: Use eDPI to maintain consistent effective sensitivity across all games.
