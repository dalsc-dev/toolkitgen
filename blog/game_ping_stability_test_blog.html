Ping vs. Jitter: What's the Difference?

Ping (Latency): The time it takes for data to travel from your PC to the server and back. A consistent 60ms ping is playable because your brain can adapt to the delay.

Jitter: The variation in your ping. If your ping jumps from 20ms to 80ms and back within seconds, that is High Jitter. Game engines struggle to interpolate player positions during jitter spikes, causing characters to teleport or bullets to "ghost" through enemies.

Packet Loss: When data fails to reach the destination entirely. Even 1% packet loss can cause critical inputs (like using an ultimate ability) to be ignored by the server.

The Network Stability Score Formula

Score = 100 - (Jitter × 2) - (Loss % × 10) - (Max(0, Ping - 50) × 0.5)

How the Test Works

Since browsers block direct ICMP (Ping) requests for security, this tool uses HTTP Burst Traffic. It sends rapid, lightweight requests to high-performance CDNs in your selected region:

  • Burst Mode: We send 5 requests per second (5Hz) to stress-test similarly to game servers
  • Jitter Calculation: Standard deviation of the last 60 response times
  • Loss Detection: Requests over 800ms are flagged as "dropped packets"

The Impact of Tick Rate

Modern competitive shooters like Valorant and CS2 operate at high tick rates (64Hz or 128Hz). This means the server updates the game state up to 128 times per second. If your Jitter is high, your client sends data intervals out of sync with the server's tick window.

Bufferbloat Warning

If your ping spikes significantly while others stream Netflix, you suffer from Bufferbloat. This tool visualizes those spikes on the real-time graph. Enabling "QoS" (Quality of Service) on your router is the best fix.

Interpreting Your "Safe to Play" Score

  • 90-100 (Safe to Play): Low ping, near-zero jitter. Perfect for ranked
  • 70-89 (Playable): Occasional spikes, minor hit-reg issues
  • Below 70 (Risk of Loss): Unstable connection, expect rubber-banding

Network congestion, WiFi interference, bufferbloat, and ISP issues.

4. What is packet loss?

When data packets fail to reach the server. Even 1% can cause missed inputs.

Yes, WiFi introduces more jitter than wired. Use ethernet when possible.
