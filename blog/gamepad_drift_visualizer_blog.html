The Circularity Equation

The Circularity Error (E) is the absolute difference between reported vector magnitude and theoretical unit circle radius:

E = |1 - √(x² + y²)| × 100%

A result of 0% indicates a perfect circle. Most controllers show errors between 5% and 20% due to "Square Gating."

The Anatomy of Stick Drift

Potentiometers vs. Hall Effect

Potentiometers: Most controllers use these mechanical components. Over time, microscopic carbon dust accumulates, causing "phantom" voltage - the controller reports movement when you're not touching the stick.

Hall Effect: High-end controllers use magnets and sensors that never physically touch. Zero friction means no mechanical wear and surgically clean "return to center."

Understanding the Visualizer Trace

  • The Centering Snap: Move stick to edge and let it snap back. Does the dot consistently land left of center? Your centering spring has "taken a set."
  • Square vs. Circular Gating: Push to corners - if you hit (1.0, 1.0), your controller is Square Gated with high circularity error.

DIY Maintenance & Troubleshooting

  1. Isopropyl Purge: Use 99% isopropyl alcohol on a cotton swab around the stick base, rotate for 60 seconds.
  2. Compressed Air Flush: Blow out debris after alcohol evaporates.
  3. Deadzone Calibration: Set your average offset as "Inner Deadzone" in game settings.

Platform Specific Advice

  • Xbox Series X/S: Polls at 125Hz wireless, up to 250Hz via USB
  • PlayStation 5 (DualSense): Bluetooth polling up to 500Hz+
  • Nintendo Switch (Joy-Cons): Notorious for drift due to low-profile design

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Why does my controller only show up after I press a button?

This is browser security - press any button (A, X, Start) to authorize Gamepad API access.

2. What is a good circularity error score?

8-15% is normal for standard controllers. Below 1% is excellent for Hall Effect controllers.

3. Does this work on Android?

Yes. Android supports Xbox and PS controllers via Bluetooth or USB-OTG.

4. What causes stick drift?

Worn potentiometers, carbon dust accumulation, or damaged springs.

5. What is Hall Effect drift?

Nearly non-existent - these sensors don't wear from friction.

6. How do I fix drift?

Clean with isopropyl alcohol, increase deadzone, or replace the controller.

7. What is polling rate?

How often the controller reports position - higher Hz means lower input lag.

8. What is input lag?

The delay between moving the stick and the game registering the movement.

9. What is a "dead zone"?

The area near center where the controller ignores input to prevent drift.

10. Does cleaning always work?

No - if the potentiometer track is physically worn, replacement is needed.

11. What controller has no drift?

Hall Effect controllers like GuliKit, 8BitDo Ultimate, or GameSir T4.

12. What is WebHID?

Browser API for accessing gamepad input directly.

13. What is circularity error?

Deviation from a perfect 1.0 radius circle when moving diagonally.

14. Does cable matter?

Wired can provide more stable polling than wireless.

15. What games are most affected?

Fighting games, FPS, and precision racers are most sensitive to drift.

16. Can drift be permanent?

Yes - worn potentiometers won't recover with cleaning.

17. What is X/Y offset?

The constant error value when the stick is at rest.

18. Which stick is most important?

Left stick for movement, right stick for aiming - both matter.

19. How accurate is this test?

Very accurate - it reads raw hardware values before software processing.

20. Can drift affect aim assist?

Yes - inconsistent input interferes with aim assist systems.

21. Does firmware affect drift?

Rarely - drift is almost always a hardware issue.

22. What is stick tension?

The resistance you feel when moving the stick - affects precision feel.

23. Should I use trigger stops?

For precision gaming, yes - they define the exact activation point.

24. Does controller age matter?

Yes - potentiometer controllers typically show drift after 1-2 years.

25. What is jitter cloud?

The visible spread of points when the stick is at rest - shows noise level.
