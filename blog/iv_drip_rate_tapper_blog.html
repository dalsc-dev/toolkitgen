Understanding IV Therapy Basics

Intravenous (IV) therapy delivers medication directly into the bloodstream through veins. This method provides rapid medication absorption, making it essential for many medical treatments. Accurate drip rates are critical for patient safety and therapeutic effectiveness.

IV therapy is used for:

  • Fluid replacement
  • Medication administration
  • Nutrition delivery
  • Blood product transfusion
  • Electrolyte management

Key IV Terminology

Understanding key terms is essential:

  • Drop Factor (DF) - Drops per mL the tubing delivers
  • mL/hr - Flow rate in milliliters per hour
  • gtt/mL - Drops per milliliter
  • Dose - Amount of medication per weight/time
  • Concentration - Amount of drug per volume of fluid

IV Tubing and Drop Factors

Different tubing delivers different drop rates:

Macrodrip Tubing

  • Standard: 10, 15, or 20 gtt/mL
  • Used for routine IV administration
  • Room available sets: 10, 12, 15, 20

Microdrip Tubing

  • 60 gtt/mL
  • Used for precise/pediatric dosing
  • Also called pediatric or minidrip

The IV Drip Rate Formula

The basic formula for drip rate calculation:

gtts/min = (Volume × Drop Factor) ÷ (Time in minutes)

This can be rearranged depending on what you know:

When calculating mL/hr:

mL/hr = (gtt/min × 60) ÷ Drop Factor

When calculating for specific dose:

First: Determine concentration, then flow rate

Step-by-Step Calculations

1. Simple Volume/Time Calculations

To deliver 1000mL over 8 hours with 20 gtt/mL tubing:

  • 1000 mL ÷ 8 hr = 125 mL/hr
  • 125 × 20 ÷ 60 = 41.67 gtt/min
  • Round to 42 gtt/min

2. Medication-Based Calculations

To deliver dopamine at 5 mcg/kg/min for a 70kg patient:

  • Dose: 5 × 70 = 350 mcg/min
  • Using 400mg in 250mL (1600 mcg/mL)
  • 350 ÷ 1600 = 0.22 mL/min
  • 0.22 × 60 = 13.1 mL/hr

3. Heparin Calculations

Standard heparin infusion: 25,000 units in 250mL:

  • 100 units/mL concentration
  • Example: 1000 units/hr = 10 mL/hr

Common IV Medications and Typical Rates

Standard medications and ranges:

Heparin

Typical: 10-25 units/kg/hr; concentration: 25,000 units/250mL

Nitroglycerin

Typical: 5-400 mcg/min; concentration: 50mg/250mL

Nicardipine

Typical: 5-15 mg/hr; concentration: 2mg/mL

Dopamine

Typical: 2-20 mcg/kg/min; concentration: 400mg/250mL

Epinephrine

Typical: 1-10 mcg/min; concentration: 1mg/250mL

Safety Considerations

Patient safety is paramount:

1. Double-Check Calculations

Always verify calculations. Use two nurses when available.

2. Start Low, Go Slow

Many medications start at lower rates and titrate upward.

3. Monitor Response

Vital signs and response affect titration.

4. Know Maximum Rates

Never exceed recommended rates without orders.

5. Document Everything

Accurate documentation prevents errors.

Troubleshooting Common Issues

Not Dripping

Check: pump settings, tubing position, vein integrity, clamp position

Running Too Fast

Check: pump programming, patient position, tubing type

Infiltration

Signs: swelling, pain, decreased flow, cooler extremity

Catheter Problems

Multiple issues can cause poor flow; assess and troubleshoot systematically

Pump Programming Basics

Modern IV pumps require careful programming:

  • Verify concentration in pump matches medication
  • Confirm weight is correctly entered
  • Verify dose units match order
  • Check rate limits are appropriate
  • Confirm bag volume matches pump programming

Special Populations

Pediatric Patients

Use microdrip tubing. Lower doses. More precise calculations.

Renal Impairment

Adjust doses/rates based on kidney function.

Cardiac Patients

Monitor hemodynamics closely. Start low.

Elderly Patients

Lower volumes and rates typically required.

Common Formula Reference

  • gtt/min = (mL/hr × gtt/mL) ÷ 60
  • mL/hr = (gtt/min × 60) ÷ gtt/mL
  • Total time = (Volume × DF) ÷ (gtts/min × 60)
  • Dose rate = Weight × Desired dose × 60 ÷ Concentration

FAQ: IV Drip Rates

1. What is drop factor?

Drops per mL the tubing delivers.

2. When do I use microdrip?

For pediatric patients or precise dosing.

3. What is a normal drip rate?

Varies by medication. Check order.

4. How do I calculate from mg/hr?

Convert mg to mL using concentration, then calculate rate.

5. Why accuracy matters?

IV medications act immediately; errors have immediate effects.

6. What is titration?

Adjusting rate based on patient response.

7. When should I double-check?

Always, particularly high-risk medications.

8. What's a pump?

Device that controls IV flow precisely.

9. What if pump alarms?

Check for upstream occlusion, empty bag, etc.

10. Can I convert gtt to mL/hr?

Yes: Multiply gtt/min by 60, divide by drop factor.
