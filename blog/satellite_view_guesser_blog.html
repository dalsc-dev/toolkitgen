What is Where on Earth?

Where on Earth? is an engaging browser-based geography game that presents players with satellite images of locations worldwide. Your mission: identify where in the world each image depicts. The game utilizes real satellite and aerial photography to create a challenging, educational experience that sharpens geographic awareness and reveals the surprising beauty of our planet from above.

The appeal of this game lies in its unique perspective. Most of us navigate the world at ground level, seeing only small portions of our surroundings. Where on Earth? flips this paradigm, revealing how recognizable—or unrecognizable—familiar places appear when viewed from orbit.

How the Game Works

The gameplay of Where on Earth? is elegantly simple yet endlessly engaging:

Image Presentation

Players are shown a satellite image, often cropped to remove identifying context. The image might show coastlines, rivers, mountains, urban patterns, agricultural fields, or natural formations. Difficulty varies based on how much context is visible.

Multiple Choice or Free Response

Depending on the game mode, players either select from multiple location options or input their guess directly. Multiple choice provides hints through proximity to correct answers, while free response tests pure knowledge.

Scoring System

Points are awarded based on accuracy and speed. Faster, more accurate guesses earn higher scores. The scoring encourages both geographic knowledge and quick pattern recognition.

Progressive Difficulty

Early rounds might show famous landmarks or easily recognizable features. As players progress, images become more obscure, requiring deeper geographic knowledge and sharper observation skills.

The Geography of Recognition

Understanding how satellite recognition works enhances gameplay:

Landmark Identification

Some features are distinctive from space—broad urban patterns, unique coastlines, major rivers, and famous structures. Learning to spot these provides reliable anchor points.

Scale and Context

Recognizing the scale of features helps narrow possibilities. The size of agricultural fields, the spacing of urban areas, and the patterns of infrastructure all provide clues.

Color and Texture

Vegetation colors indicate climate zones. Urban textures vary by region. Desert versus forest versus ice reveals geographic zones even without specific location knowledge.

Geometric Patterns

Human-made features have distinct patterns—grid versus organic street layouts, irrigation circles, highway systems. These patterns often betray regional building traditions.

Why Satellite Views Are So Challenging

What makes Where on Earth? surprisingly difficult?

The Familiar Made Strange

Seeing locations from above often removes the contextual clues we use at ground level. The Eiffel Tower is obvious from ground level, but from space, the Champ de Mars looks like any urban park.

Human Perception Limitations

Our brains evolved for ground-level navigation, not aerial interpretation. Satellite viewing requires developing entirely new visual processing skills.

Global Similarity

Deserts look like deserts anywhere. Agricultural fields have similar patterns globally. Without distinctive markers, distinguishing Australian outback from African savanna requires subtle clues.

Intentional Difficulty

Game designers deliberately crop and angle images to maximize challenge. Perfectly recognizable locations become detective puzzles.

Key Geographic Patterns to Learn

Successful players develop mental catalogs of recognizable patterns:

Urban Signatures

American suburbs feature grid patterns with detached houses and swimming pools. European cities show denser development with older architectural patterns. Asian megacities display intense vertical development and distinctive signage even from above.

Agricultural Patterns

Center pivot irrigation creates distinctive circular fields common in American agricultural regions. Rice terraces show stepped contours in Asian mountainous areas. Vineyard patterns differ by regional planting traditions.

Coastal Features

Beach orientations, harbor structures, and coastal development patterns vary by region and development history.

Natural Formations

River delta patterns, mountain range shapes, volcanic features, and forest boundaries all provide location clues for attentive players.

Strategies for Success

Improve your satellite guessing with these proven strategies:

Start Broad, Narrow Down

Begin by identifying climate zone and continent. Is this tropical, arid, temperate, or polar? This immediately narrows possibilities dramatically.

Look for Water

Water bodies—oceans, seas, lakes, rivers—are often easier to identify and provide major orientation clues.

Assess Human Development Density

The amount and pattern of human structures indicates economic development level and urbanization patterns typical of different regions.

Note Colors and Vegetation

Green intensity, brown/arid areas, snow coverage, and vegetation patterns all indicate climate and season.

Study Infrastructure

Road patterns, highway types, railway lines, and port facilities reveal regional building traditions and economic activities.
